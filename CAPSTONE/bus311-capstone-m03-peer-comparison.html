1. Compare one annual snapshot

Use the same approved company and choose two publicly traded peers. Explain one useful business-model/customer/product similarity and one limitation for each peer. Familiarity or membership in a broad sector alone is not enough.

Use one recent completed annual period for each company and show exact fiscal year ends. Prefer the same fiscal year where practical. Use the same ratio definitions and units. State important period or business-model differences. The selected-peer median uses only the two peers; it is not an industry average.

On Peer comparison, enter company names/tickers in B8:D8, exact annual year ends in B9:D9, and statement currency/scale in B10:D10. Enter five annual ratios in B12:D16. You may retrieve the ratios from FactSet or credible reports rather than calculate every ratio from raw statements. Use annual historical ratios, not forward estimates. Record the report/metric, period, definition and useful location in the sources response.

  • Operating margin = operating income / revenue. It shows operating profit per sales dollar. Compare business mix and unusual items before attributing a difference to management quality.
  • Total asset turnover = revenue / average total assets. Average total assets = (beginning assets + ending assets) / 2. One annual turnover ratio uses the asset balances at the beginning and end of that year. A high turnover may reflect business structure rather than superior performance.
  • Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities. It indicates short-term balance-sheet coverage, not cash available to spend. Inventory quality, timing, and business model matter.
  • Debt-to-assets = short- and long-term interest-bearing borrowings / ending total assets. Use borrowings rather than total liabilities. For this exercise exclude lease liabilities, applying that definition consistently to all companies; explain any source-definition difference.
  • Choose one fifth ratio relevant to the business. Inventory turnover = cost of goods sold / average inventory. Receivables turnover = revenue / average net receivables when credit sales are unavailable, with that proxy disclosed. ROA = net income / average total assets. Other measures require instructor approval. Document the numerator, denominator, averaging, and units.

Provider labels need checking. Some debt ratios use total liabilities or include leases; some turnover ratios use ending assets. Use the definitions above consistently. If the provider measure differs, find a compatible field, calculate the intended measure or obtain an approved substitute. Enter a percentage as 12% (or 0.12), not as 12. Enter times as numbers, such as 2.00. State the chosen fifth ratio and unit in its response and F16.

Calculate the two-peer medians in E12:E16, using MEDIAN of columns C:D on the same row. Both peer values must be available. If one is missing, report n.a. and explain the limitation; do not silently present a single-peer value as a two-peer median.

Verify one company ratio yourself. Choose any of the five ratios. Name it in B38, enter its numerator in B41 and denominator in B42, and calculate B43 = B41/B42. Use an average balance when required. Copy the reported company ratio into B44, apply the correct unit/format and explain any rounding or definition difference in B45. A zero denominator is unavailable, not a valid zero result.

Write three short conclusions: one strength, one constraint, and what the comparison means for your proposed idea. Use numerical comparisons and business reasoning. Higher is not always better, and current ratio does not measure cash available to spend.

For banks, insurers, REITs or other businesses where a default ratio is unsuitable, obtain approval for a relevant replacement. Keep five meaningful comparison positions with consistent labels, definitions and units. Do not force an unsuitable metric or invent missing values.

2. Make one brief P/E estimate

On P-E check, state one common price observation date in B5. Enter each price in B11:D11, compatible reported/GAAP diluted trailing EPS in B12:D12, and the earnings period end dates in B13:D13. TTM and LTM both mean the most recent twelve months. Retrieve trailing EPS directly when available; reconstructing it is not required. Check the field definition rather than assuming every EPS label means reported diluted earnings.

Calculate three P/Es in B15:D15. Then calculate one selected-peer median P/E in B20, one implied company price in B21, and one percentage difference in B22. Use your company's EPS and observed price, not a peer's, for the implied price and percentage comparison.

  • P/E = observed share price / positive trailing-12-month diluted EPS. Use the same price date for all three companies and earnings available on that date. Record each TTM end date. Match price/EPS currency and share class.
  • Use MEDIAN of the two positive peer P/Es for one benchmark. With two observations median equals mean. Implied company price = peer median P/E × your company EPS. Percentage difference = implied price / observed company price - 1.
  • If either peer multiple is unavailable or not positive, a complete two-peer benchmark is unavailable. Zero/negative company EPS makes P/E and the implied estimate not meaningful. Discuss an alternative with the instructor.
  • Low/high estimates using the smaller/larger peer P/E are optional enrichment, not required work or extra credit. The required result is one median-based estimate and its interpretation.
  • Business mix, growth, risk and unusual earnings can limit comparability. This is market context, not a project valuation, NPV or definitive fair value.

Explain what the estimate suggests and one limitation in B28. Name the price and EPS evidence in B25. Record any EPS exception in B31. A defensible conclusion may be that the method has limited usefulness for this company.

3. Ask AI to red-team the proposed revenue idea

Use AI challenge to bring forward the Milestone 2 idea and paying customer. Give AI a short summary you wrote from public information, including the company context, proposed idea, one financial strength and one constraint. The required challenge concerns the revenue idea itself, not only a ratio calculation or peer choice.

Act as a skeptical CFO. Challenge my proposed revenue idea using the company context and financial findings below. Identify weaknesses in customer demand, pricing, displaced existing sales, costs and execution. Give me three important objections and one practical test. Distinguish questions from facts and do not invent company information.

Record your actual prompt in B8 and summarize the strongest objection in B11. Do not paste the whole conversation. In B14, independently check one important criticism using a company filing, credible public evidence or your own calculation. Name what you checked, what you found and any remaining uncertainty. Another AI answer is not an independent check. If evidence is inconclusive, explain that honestly and propose a practical test.

In B17, accept, modify or reject the objection and explain your specific revision or justified retention of the idea. Choose Pursue, Pilot, or Strengthen first in B20. In B23 state a next action, responsible role, timeframe, success measure and observable reconsideration trigger. Label proposed thresholds as assumptions. Disclose the AI tool, date and purpose in B26.

Finish with a 100-150-word recommendation in B29 connecting your idea, one strength, one constraint, the P/E result and limitation, the checked AI objection, and your next step. Your reasoning determines the recommendation; AI does not make the decision. If access fails, contact the instructor for an alternative.

Give public AI tools only a short summary you wrote from public information. Do not upload workbooks, raw FactSet exports or screenshots, personal information, private teaching material, or nonpublic information. If AI is used, name the tool and purpose in the workbook revision response and disclose it briefly in the presentation appendix or source notes.

Students and the instructor may use FactSet evidence in BUS311 course deliverables. Raw exports and screenshots remain in approved course-restricted systems and are not committed publicly or uploaded to public AI tools.

Suggested class sequence

  1. Peer snapshot, 20 minutes. Check peer choices, periods and definitions; calculate medians and verify one ratio.
  2. P/E, 10 minutes. Calculate and interpret the one estimate.
  3. AI red team, 10 minutes. Run the prompt, choose an objection and begin the independent check.
  4. Recommendation, 10 minutes. Revise the idea and define a measurable next step.

Gather the financial data before class where possible. Complete missing research and the final writing afterward.
