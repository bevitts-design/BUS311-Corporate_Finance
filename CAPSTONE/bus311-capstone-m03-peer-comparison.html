Select two useful peers

Choose publicly traded businesses with similar products, customers, business models, or operating economics. Explain why each is useful and name one important difference. Avoid selecting a company solely because it has a familiar name or belongs to the same broad sector.

Use three comparable company fiscal years and the latest reasonably comparable completed year for each peer. Show exact year ends. Use the same accounting/ratio definitions and appropriate average balances. Name the actual selected-peer median, not an industry average. Two peers are a small descriptive sample.

For banks, insurers, REITs, or companies where the default ratios are not meaningful, obtain approval for suitable substitutions before calculation. Keep the five comparison positions, but update labels, input definitions, formulas, and the chart consistently. Do not force an unsuitable metric or invent missing data.

Build the company trend

On Company ratios, enter years in B10:D10, exact year ends in B11:D11, and financial inputs in B12:D20. Columns are labeled oldest, middle, and latest fiscal year. Use the stated currency and scale consistently. Enter genuine zero values as 0; do not turn missing data into zero.

Calculate average assets in B23:D23 and the five ratios in B27:D31. For the middle and latest years, the beginning asset balance normally equals the previous year’s ending balance. Explain restatements or other differences.

  • Operating margin = operating income / revenue. It shows operating profit per sales dollar. Compare business mix and unusual items before attributing a difference to management quality.
  • Total asset turnover = revenue / average total assets. Average total assets = (beginning assets + ending assets) / 2. Three flow years therefore require four year-end asset balances. A high turnover may reflect business structure rather than superior performance.
  • Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities. It indicates short-term balance-sheet coverage, not cash available to spend. Inventory quality, timing, and business model matter.
  • Debt-to-assets = short- and long-term interest-bearing borrowings / ending total assets. Use borrowings rather than total liabilities. For this exercise exclude lease liabilities, applying that definition consistently to all companies; explain any source-definition difference.
  • Choose one fifth ratio relevant to the business. Inventory turnover = cost of goods sold / average inventory. Receivables turnover = revenue / average net receivables when credit sales are unavailable, with that proxy disclosed. ROA = net income / average total assets. Other measures require instructor approval. Document the numerator, denominator, averaging, and units.

The fifth ratio uses row 19 for its numerator and row 20 for its denominator. If the denominator must be an average, calculate that average from the supporting balances and explain those balances in your definition response. Keep its definition and units consistent in every year and for both peers. Format the fifth-ratio cells appropriately: percent, times, or days.

For example, oldest-year operating margin uses B13/B12, asset turnover uses B12/B23, current ratio uses B14/B15, and debt-to-assets uses B18/B17. Use cell references. A zero or missing denominator makes a ratio unavailable, not a valid zero result. Explain the limitation using n.a. rather than hiding an error.

Compare financial performance

On Peer comparison, enter peer names in C8:D8 and latest comparable fiscal-year inputs in C10:D20. Your company’s latest-year inputs link automatically from Company ratios. Use the same financial definitions for all three companies.

Calculate peer average assets in C23:D23, peer ratios in C27:D31, and the selected-peer median in E27:E31 using MEDIAN of the two peer results in columns C and D. If either peer ratio is unavailable, explain the gap before reporting a two-peer median. Column B links to the company’s latest ratios.

The editable chart below the comparison links to the three current ratios. It fills when you enter your formulas. Interpret it in the chart response. If current ratio is unsuitable and you have an approved substitution, update the chart’s source metric, title, and unit together.

Discuss all five ratios through the profitability, efficiency, and liquidity/financing prompts. State the values, a plausible explanation, and what the difference means for the Milestone 2 opportunity. Higher is not automatically better, and a small difference may have little practical significance.

Complete the brief P/E check

Use one common share-price observation date and earnings information available on that date. Financial-statement ratios use annual periods; the P/E exercise uses the separately labeled trailing-12-month EPS period. Do not mix annual EPS for one company with forward EPS for another.

  • P/E = observed share price / trailing-12-month diluted EPS. Use the same share-price observation date for all three companies and earnings information available on that date. Record each TTM end date. Currency units must match within each price/EPS pair.
  • Calculate the two peer P/Es. Low = the smaller positive peer P/E; base = MEDIAN of the two positive peer P/Es; high = the larger. With two observations, the median equals their mean. These are illustrative comparison cases, not forecasts or confidence bounds.
  • Illustrative company value per share = selected peer P/E × company trailing EPS. Percentage difference from observed price = implied price / observed company price − 1. Keep the calculation in unscaled per-share currency, even if statement inputs are in millions.
  • Both peers must have usable positive P/Es for this two-peer range. A zero/negative EPS makes P/E not meaningful (n.m.). Missing data is unavailable, not zero. Seek an approved alternative when needed and explain the limitation. Do not silently omit a peer or apply a negative multiple.
  • Explain what the estimate assumes about comparability. Historical earnings, unusual items, different growth prospects, and a small peer sample can change the conclusion. The P/E calculation does not determine the NPV or value created by the proposed growth project.

On P-E check, enter prices in B11:D11, diluted TTM EPS in B12:D12, and TTM end dates in B13:D13. Calculate company and peer P/Es in B15:D15. For the illustrative range, enter low/base/high peer multiples in B20:D20, implied company share prices in B21:D21, and percentage differences in B22:D22.

Use the smaller, median, and larger values of the two peer multiples in C15:D15. Multiply each selected multiple by the project company’s EPS in B12, not a peer EPS. Compare each implied price with the project company’s observed price in B11. Name price/EPS sources and explain material unusual earnings effects.

Equivalent valid formulas are accepted. A defensible conclusion can be that this method has limited usefulness for your company. Explain why and use an instructor-approved alternative when required.

Revise the growth recommendation

Return to Recommendation. Identify one financial strength and one constraint, each tied to a specific result. Explain a concrete change to the proposal or a justified decision to retain it.

RecommendationMeaning
PursueThe evidence supports moving to the next implementation step, with stated conditions.
PilotThe idea is promising, but demand, costs, or execution need a limited test.
Strengthen firstAddress a financial or operating constraint before expanding.

Use a peer, self-review, or AI for one skeptical challenge. Independently check a filing/report or recalculate the concern. Record accept, modify, or reject and explain the response. Specify the next action, responsible role, timeframe, success measure, and a condition that would cause you to reconsider.

Finish with a 100–150-word recommendation. Use ratios and the P/E result as evidence while explaining their limits. A share-price estimate does not prove the growth initiative creates value or that the company has cash to fund it.

Give public AI tools only a short summary you wrote from public information. Do not upload workbooks, raw FactSet exports or screenshots, personal information, private teaching material, or nonpublic information. If AI is used, name the tool and purpose in the workbook revision response and disclose it briefly in the presentation appendix or source notes.

A class work sequence

  1. Peer choices, 8 minutes. Compare business models with a partner and identify one limitation for each peer.
  2. Ratio analysis, 20 minutes. Check the company trend and calculate the latest-year peer comparison. Write one strength and one constraint.
  3. P/E check, 12 minutes. Verify dates and earnings definitions, calculate the range, and interpret it.
  4. CFO challenge, 10 minutes. Challenge one conclusion, check evidence, and revise the recommendation.

Collect the necessary financial inputs before this work session where possible. Finish missing research, calculations, and writing afterward.
