Common Stage 2 edge cases

Fewer than three defensible peers

Use the defensible peers available and explain why additional companies are not sufficiently comparable.

A segment, market-share measure, or driver is not disclosed

State the limitation, use a supportable proxy only when its definition is clear, and never invent a figure.

Definitions changed across years

Reconcile or disclose restatements, acquisitions, divestitures, fiscal-year changes, and segment-definition changes before comparing growth.

A driver cannot be quantified

Classify it as directional, cite the supporting evidence, and state what would be needed to quantify it.

Using AI or the non-AI path

Either path must produce the same skeptical-CFO challenge record. AI output is not evidence, and raw FactSet files or screenshots may not be uploaded to public AI tools.

Frequently asked questions

What is the goal of Stage 1?

Explore one approved public company, learn the basics through AI and/or ordinary web search plus required FactSet research, record one concrete FactSet learning and the screen, report, or feature used, and propose one potential CFO decision.

Do I need to prove or validate my Stage 1 idea?

No. Stage 1 is exploratory. You do not yet need to verify the recommendation, validate a hypothesis, compare or defend alternatives, build an evidence register, complete formal citations, or obtain a formal approval decision. Those tasks belong in later stages.

What exactly do I submit for Stage 2?

Upload one required file: the completed BUS311 Capstone Revenue Analysis Workbook (.xlsx), including the Hypothesis & Scenarios sheet. Submit a separate PDF or DOCX only if Canvas or the instructor explicitly requests an additional readable export.

What if I cannot find three peers or the company does not disclose a segment, market-share measure, or driver?

Use the defensible evidence available and state the limitation. Explain why fewer peers are appropriate, use a supportable proxy only when its definition is clear, classify an unquantifiable driver as directional, and never invent a figure.

Does PASS on the workbook mean I earned full credit?

No. PASS on Checks & Submission confirms structural completion only. The rubric still evaluates the quality, comparability, traceability, analysis, hypothesis, and student judgment.

Is this a group project?

No. Research, evidence selection, modeling, writing, slides, the Oral Presentation, and responses to live questions are individual work.

Is a Buy, Hold, or Sell recommendation enough?

No. Market valuation may support the analysis, but the project requires one company-specific action the CFO could recommend to the Board.

Do I need a paid AI plan?

No. Each checkpoint is designed for free text chat or an instructor-provided noncommercial equivalent. Browsing, uploads, connectors, image generation, and spreadsheet analysis are not required.

Can I upload FactSet files or screenshots to a public AI tool?

No. Keep raw FactSet exports and screenshots in Canvas Course Files or another course-restricted location. Use only sanitized, student-written summaries in public AI tools.

Is AI output a source?

No. Verify retained claims with SEC filings, permitted FactSet evidence, Excel calculations, or course concepts, then record an accept, modify, or reject judgment.

What happens to the Excel model at the end?

The working Excel model is submitted and assessed at Milestone 3. It is not uploaded again for Stage 5, but you must be able to explain its logic and locate supporting evidence during the live Oral Presentation.

What do I upload and what do I present for Stage 5?

Upload exactly one editable PowerPoint company-analysis project (.pptx) by the November 30 file lock. Present that submitted deck live to the class on your assigned date. The upload is worth 50 points and the live Oral Presentation is worth 25 points.

Which dates and submission links control if something changes?

Stages 1-4 and the Stage 5 PowerPoint upload are scheduled for 12:30 p.m. ET. Canvas is the authority for the live upload link, Oral Presentation order, and any later instructor-announced change.

Student progress checklist

  1. For Stage 1, I selected one approved U.S. public company and recorded its name, ticker, and exchange.
  2. I used AI and/or ordinary web search for introductory company research and understand that these notes are exploratory, not verified evidence.
  3. I used FactSet, wrote one specific thing I learned, and named the screen, report, or feature I used.
  4. I proposed one potential CFO decision in my own words and explained briefly why it seems worth investigating.
  5. I understand that Stage 1 does not require proof, hypothesis validation, alternatives, an evidence register, formal citations, or a formal approval gate.
  6. Beginning in Stage 2, my evidence register identifies each SEC filing, FactSet item, period, units, definition, location, use, and limitation.
  7. My revenue map explains products or services, segments, geographies, customers, channels, pricing, and recurring or transactional revenue where relevant.
  8. My three-to-five-year revenue history uses comparable periods and reconciles reporting changes.
  9. My testable hypothesis names the driver, expected effect, period, and evidence that could support or falsify it.
  10. My base, upside, and downside cases flow from the revenue diagnosis.
  11. I reviewed the workbook's Checks & Submission sheet and understand that PASS confirms structural completion, not analytical quality or full credit.
  12. I will upload one completed Revenue Analysis Workbook (.xlsx) for Stage 2; I will provide a separate readable export only if Canvas or the instructor explicitly requests one.
  13. My Excel model separates inputs, sources, calculations, outputs, checks, scenarios, and sensitivities.
  14. I completed all four C-A-P-A-J checkpoints using free text chat or the instructor-provided equivalent.
  15. I independently verified every retained AI claim and recorded accept, modify, or reject judgment.
  16. My PowerPoint company-analysis project states the decision, revenue and valuation logic, alternatives, actions, risks, limitations, source notes, and verified AI-use disclosure.
  17. My submitted .pptx contains eight to ten editable core slides; optional appendix slides support analytical depth and live questions.
  18. I can present the decision in up to seven minutes and respond to live questions for up to three minutes without relying on AI; both are graded together in the 25-point Oral Presentation criterion.
  19. My one final Stage 5 filename follows the required pattern and the editable .pptx is uploaded in Canvas before the file lock.
