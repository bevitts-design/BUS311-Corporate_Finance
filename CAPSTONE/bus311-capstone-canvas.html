Milestone 4: Final PowerPoint and oral presentation

Synthesize the work already completed in Milestones 1-3. Build a clear company-specific recommendation rather than reproduce every worksheet response. Submit one editable PowerPoint and use that same file for the oral presentation.

7 core slides plus optional supporting appendix. Final PowerPoint: 50 points. Live presentation: 25 points, up to seven minutes plus three minutes of questions. Keep the existing final deadline of Monday, Nov. 30, 2026 at 12:30 p.m. ET, when class begins unless Canvas announces a change.

  1. Company and revenue engine. Introduce the company, ticker, and brief business snapshot. Explain who pays, what they buy, the sales channel, how the company charges, and what creates repeat revenue. Name the largest disclosed revenue source with its amount and fiscal year. Distinguish the payer from the user when relevant.
  2. Revenue history and drivers. Show three completed fiscal years of revenue and growth, and explain two company-specific drivers. Use a readable three-year revenue chart with currency, units, fiscal years, and relevant comparability limitations. Separate supported drivers from assumptions.
  3. Proposed revenue opportunity. Name the paying customer, willingness to pay, company fit, and whether the idea is your proposal, an announced initiative, or an extension of an existing activity. Use two pieces of company evidence supporting fit. Show a period-consistent annual revenue estimate with quantity, revenue per unit, adoption timing and displaced sales. Identify major operating costs and upfront investment, and distinguish revenue from profit and cash flow.
  4. Peer comparison. Introduce two defensible peers and show a five-ratio annual snapshot for the company and peers. Explain one strength, one constraint, and the implication for the proposed idea. Use one recent completed annual period per company, consistent definitions and units, exact year ends, and the selected-peer median. Briefly identify why the peers are useful and a material comparison limitation.
  5. Brief P/E check. Explain the one peer-median implied company share price, compare it with the observed price, and state a meaningful limitation. Show the common price date, compatible trailing diluted EPS periods, the two peer P/Es, selected median, implied company price and percentage difference. Explain an approved alternative if P/E is not meaningful.
  6. AI challenge and revision. Summarize the strongest AI objection to the proposed revenue idea, what you independently checked, and your accept, modify, or reject judgment. Name the evidence or calculation used to check the concern and explain the resulting change or justified retention of the idea. Make the evolution from the earlier tentative proposal clear.
  7. Final recommendation and next step. Recommend pursue, pilot, or strengthen first. Explain the choice relative to continuing the current approach and identify two key risks. Specify the next action, responsible role, timeframe, success measure and an observable reconsideration trigger. Label proposed thresholds as assumptions and acknowledge important remaining uncertainty.

Supporting detail and disclosure

  • Cite company reports, dates, units and material assumptions on the relevant slides or in an appendix. Include usable sources and disclosure of the AI tool and its role. AI output is not company evidence.
  • Use an appendix for detailed peer-selection explanations, ratio definitions, the one-ratio verification, supporting calculations, and source/AI disclosure when these would crowd the core slides. Be ready to explain them in questions.
  • The Milestone 1 FactSet learning, three initial Milestone 2 revenue ideas, and full Milestone 3 AI prompt can remain in the completed workbooks. Do not add separate core slides for every worksheet answer or paste an entire AI chat.
  • One annual peer-ratio snapshot and one median-based P/E estimate are sufficient. No new three-year ratio history, required low/high range, full valuation, DCF, DDM or NPV model is added.
  • Submit one .pptx file and present that same file live. No separate executive-summary report or duplicate presentation file is required.

Sources, individual work, and revisions

Research, assumptions, formulas, writing, and presentation are individual work. You must explain the submitted analysis yourself.

Use credible company filings, annual reports, or FactSet reports to support company facts and historical figures. Name the relevant filing or report and useful page, section, or link in your written responses. Clearly label calculations, assumptions, and interpretations. AI output is not financial evidence.

Students and the instructor may use FactSet evidence in BUS311 course deliverables. Raw exports and screenshots remain in approved course-restricted systems and are not committed publicly or uploaded to public AI tools.

Give public AI tools only a short summary you wrote from public information. Do not upload workbooks, raw FactSet exports or screenshots, personal information, private teaching material, or nonpublic information. If AI is used, name the tool and purpose in the workbook revision response and disclose it briefly in the presentation appendix or source notes.

Use the required minimum where evidence supports it. If no defensible peer or comparable historical figure exists, state why and discuss an appropriate limitation or proxy with the instructor. Never invent company facts. Label estimated project inputs as assumptions and explain their basis.
