Beginner exploration

Notice a decision the CFO might face

Use these directions as idea starters. For Stage 1, propose one potential decision; you do not need to compare alternatives, prove it, or defend a recommendation yet.

Your Board question

Given the company's revenue engine, financial position, market expectations, and risks, what specific action should the CFO recommend to the Board, and what evidence makes that decision credible and implementable?

Invest and grow

Fund the product, market, geography, capacity, or technology investment most likely to create durable value.

Includes: Growth investment · Market expectations

Fund the business and return capital

Choose the right mix of debt, liquidity, dividends, repurchases, and reinvestment.

Includes: Capital allocation · Capital structure · Payout policy

Buy, partner, or reshape

Evaluate acquisitions, partnerships, divestitures, and portfolio changes that sharpen strategic focus.

Includes: Corporate development · Portfolio strategy

Improve operating cash flow

Strengthen margins, ROIC, free cash flow, working capital, or liquidity through specific operating and capital actions.

Includes: Operating performance · Working capital

Manage financial risk

Respond to interest-rate, currency, commodity, refinancing, or concentration risk with a finance action.

Includes: Risk management

Revenue, evidence, and judgment

Make every later conclusion traceable

Beginning in Stage 2, use SEC filings and permitted FactSet evidence to diagnose the operating business, then use Excel to test what the decision could mean for value.

Revenue engine and hypothesis

  • Map products or services, segments, geographies, customers, channels, pricing, and recurring or transactional revenue.
  • Analyze three to five comparable fiscal years and reconcile reporting changes.
  • Separate company-relevant drivers such as price, volume, mix, currency, acquisitions, capacity, retention, or backlog.
  • State a testable hypothesis with a driver, expected effect, period, and falsification evidence.

Evidence register and source labels

  • Name the SEC filing, FactSet report or screen, metric, stable link, or other credible source.
  • Record the data-through or retrieval date, fiscal period, units, currency, and definition.
  • Identify the exact location and use, plus peer selection rationale and limitations when applicable.
  • Keep FactSet captures intended only for enrolled students in Canvas Course Files or another course-restricted location.

Excel milestone expectations

  • Separate inputs, sources, calculations, outputs, checks, scenarios, and sensitivities.
  • Link base, upside, and downside assumptions to the revenue hypothesis.
  • Use applicable tax, NOPAT, D&A, CapEx, working-capital, discount-rate, terminal-value, and enterprise-to-equity logic.
  • Resolve formula errors and show the assumptions most capable of changing the recommendation.

Board-ready final work

  • Use an editable 8-10-slide core PPTX with optional analytical appendix slides.
  • Create one accessible, readable, U.S.-Letter executive-summary PDF.
  • Keep decision, assumptions, results, actions, risks, limitations, and sources consistent across both files.
  • Prepare for a seven-minute briefing and three minutes of individual Q&A.

After Stage 1

Use AI to challenge—not replace—your judgment

The four C-A-P-A-J checkpoints begin after the exploratory Stage 1 brief. Complete them through the bounded AI guide, then submit the red-team record.

  1. Sanitize inputs: use student-written summaries and never upload raw FactSet files, screenshots, private information, or instructor-only materials.
  2. Challenge the model and recommendation with C-A-P-A-J during Stages 3 and 4.
  3. Verify every retained claim or calculation with SEC filings, permitted FactSet evidence, Excel, or course concepts.
  4. Record the challenge, evidence, and accept, modify, or reject judgment in the red-team record.

Frequently asked questions

Know the boundaries

These answers summarize the approved project contract. Canvas controls any later instructor-announced change.

What is the goal of Stage 1?

Explore one approved public company, learn the basics through AI and/or ordinary web search plus required FactSet research, record one concrete FactSet learning and the screen, report, or feature used, and propose one potential CFO decision.

Do I need to prove or validate my Stage 1 idea?

No. Stage 1 is exploratory. You do not yet need to verify the recommendation, validate a hypothesis, compare or defend alternatives, build an evidence register, complete formal citations, or obtain a formal approval decision. Those tasks belong in later stages.

Is this a group project?

No. Research, evidence selection, modeling, writing, slides, delivery, and Q&A are individual work.

Is a Buy, Hold, or Sell recommendation enough?

No. Market valuation may support the analysis, but the project requires one company-specific action the CFO could recommend to the Board.

Do I need a paid AI plan?

No. Each checkpoint is designed for free text chat or an instructor-provided noncommercial equivalent. Browsing, uploads, connectors, image generation, and spreadsheet analysis are not required.

Can I upload FactSet files or screenshots to a public AI tool?

No. Keep raw FactSet exports and screenshots in Canvas Course Files or another course-restricted location. Use only sanitized, student-written summaries in public AI tools.

Is AI output a source?

No. Verify retained claims with SEC filings, permitted FactSet evidence, Excel calculations, or course concepts, then record an accept, modify, or reject judgment.

What happens to the Excel model at the end?

The working Excel model is submitted and assessed at Milestone 3. It is not a third final communication file unless the instructor requests it for Q&A verification.

What are the final communication files?

Submit exactly two files: the editable PPTX Board brief and the one-page executive summary PDF.

Which dates and submission links control if something changes?

Canvas is the authority for live submission links, exact due times, presentation order, and any later instructor-announced change.

  3. I used FactSet, wrote one specific thing I learned, and named the screen, report, or feature I used.
  4. I proposed one potential CFO decision in my own words and explained briefly why it seems worth investigating.
  5. I understand that Stage 1 does not require proof, hypothesis validation, alternatives, an evidence register, formal citations, or a formal approval gate.
  6. Beginning in Stage 2, my evidence register identifies each SEC filing, FactSet item, period, units, definition, location, use, and limitation.
  7. My revenue map explains products or services, segments, geographies, customers, channels, pricing, and recurring or transactional revenue where relevant.
  8. My three-to-five-year revenue history uses comparable periods and reconciles reporting changes.
  9. My testable hypothesis names the driver, expected effect, period, and evidence that could support or falsify it.
  10. My base, upside, and downside cases flow from the revenue diagnosis.
  11. My Excel model separates inputs, sources, calculations, outputs, checks, scenarios, and sensitivities.
  12. I completed all four C-A-P-A-J checkpoints using free text chat or the instructor-provided equivalent.
  13. I independently verified every retained AI claim and recorded accept, modify, or reject judgment.
  14. My one-page executive summary and PPTX state the same decision, assumptions, results, actions, risks, and source labels.
  15. My core deck contains eight to ten editable slides; appendix slides are reserved for Q&A support.
  16. I can deliver the briefing in seven minutes and answer questions for three minutes without relying on AI.
  17. My two final filenames follow the required pattern and are submitted in Canvas before the file lock.
