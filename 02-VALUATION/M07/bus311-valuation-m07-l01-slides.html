Gordon Growth values dividends that grow at a steady rate

What does growth mean?

g is the annual percentage increase in dividends per share.

At 4% growth, each year’s dividend is the previous dividend × 1.04.

The Gordon Growth model

A shortcut for the present value of all future dividends, assuming the same growth rate continues indefinitely.

P₀ = D₁ / (r − g)

D₁ = next year’s dividend: $2.12
r = investors’ required return: 8.5%
g = steady dividend growth: 4.0%

$2.12 / (8.5% − 4.0%) = $47.11
Use for a stable dividend payer. The model requires r > g and sustainable long-run growth.
Coca-Cola 2025 Form 10-K · dividend evidence; r and g are classroom assumptions

P/E asks what investors will pay for each dollar of earnings

P/E = share price ÷ earnings per share

A 22× P/E means investors pay $22 for $1 of annual earnings. A justified peer multiple turns EPS into a price estimate.

Estimated price = EPS × selected P/E$3.04 × 22 = $66.88FY2025 diluted GAAP EPS · illustrative trailing multiple

Instead of a dividend model

If dividends are absent, irregular, or do not reflect earnings capacity, P/E can provide a market-based estimate—when EPS is positive and meaningful.

Alongside DDM / Gordon Growth

Compare the dividend-based value with how similar firms are priced. A gap prompts questions about payout, growth, risk, and peer fit.

Choose comparable firms with similar growth, margins, leverage, and accounting. Match trailing EPS with trailing P/E. A high P/E is not automatically better; negative or distorted EPS calls for another method.
EPS evidence: Coca-Cola 2025 Form 10-K · 22× is a classroom assumption

Retained earnings fund growth—but does that growth add value?

Retention: earnings kept in the company

Payout ratio = dividends ÷ earnings: the percentage paid to owners.

$3 dividend / $5 EPS = 60% payout

The company keeps $5 − $3 = $2 per share.
Retention ratio, b = earnings kept ÷ total earnings.

b = $2 / $5 = 40%

Equivalently: b = 1 − 60% = 40%.
The $2 is what stays; the $5 is all earnings.

PVGO: value added by future investment

Present Value of Growth Opportunities: the value contribution of future investments.

PVGO = P₀ − EPS₁ / r

Given: modeled price P₀ = $50,
next-year EPS = $5, required return = 12%.

No-growth value: $5 / 0.12 = $41.67

PVGO: $50 − $41.67 = $8.33

Check the decomposition$50 = $41.67 + $8.33

The $50 modeled price is supplied for this illustration. It is not calculated from retention alone.

PVGO can be positive, zero, or negative. Retention creates value only when new investment earns enough.
